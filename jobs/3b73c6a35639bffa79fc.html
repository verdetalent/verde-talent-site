Position Summary

This Senior Sourcing Specialist position is the third level of the Nuclear Sourcing classification hierarchy and is responsible for developing and executing strategic sourcing plans in support of evaluating potential new nuclear generation. This role provides an opportunity for an experienced sourcing professional to lead complex, high-value procurement activities supporting one of Duke Energy’s most significant potential capital investment programs. The position supports sourcing of materials, equipment, long-lead items, engineering, construction, fabrication, testing, logistics, warehousing, and professional services required to evaluate and potentially develop new nuclear generation.

The Senior Sourcing Specialist will lead the full strategic sourcing lifecycle, including opportunity assessment, demand and category analysis, supply-market assessment, sourcing strategy development, competitive bid events, supplier evaluations, negotiations, contract development, supplier readiness, and performance management. The position will manage complex and high-value sourcing initiatives where commercial strategy, supplier capability, schedule, quality, and risk management are critical to project success.

A successful candidate will bring strong strategic sourcing and negotiation experience, commercial judgment, analytical capability, and the ability to influence stakeholders across a complex enterprise. The individual will work proactively with the Nuclear Business Unit, Project Management & Construction (PMC), Nuclear Operations, Engineering, Project Controls, Legal, Finance, Treasury/Tax, Cybersecurity, Credit Risk, Insurance, Quality/Nuclear Oversight, and supplier stakeholders to develop practical sourcing solutions and build alignment across organizations.

The ideal candidate is a proactive sourcing professional who can translate technical and project requirements into effective commercial strategies, challenge assumptions, identify opportunities, manage supplier and commercial risk, and communicate recommendations clearly to project and executive leadership. Employees at this level are fully competent in applicable sourcing concepts and procedures, work independently on most assignments, identify problems, develop solutions, and escalate unusual or complex issues appropriately.

Responsibilities

  • Sourcing Strategy Development: Develop and present sourcing strategies for complex, high-value materials, equipment, engineering, construction, fabrication, testing, transportation, warehousing, and professional services. Evaluate competitive sourcing options, market conditions, supplier capabilities, project requirements, commercial alternatives, and risk to recommend sourcing approaches that support project schedule, quality, cost, and enterprise objectives.
  • Strategy Execution and Governance: Lead end-to-end sourcing activities including RFIs, RFPs, competitive bid events, supplier evaluations, commercial evaluations, award recommendations, governance reviews, contract releases, purchase order releases, and supplier onboarding. Establish clear evaluation criteria, document sourcing decisions, coordinate required approvals, and ensure effective handoffs to contract management, supplier performance management, project controls, and execution teams.
  • Negotiation and Commercial Value Delivery: Develop negotiation strategies and lead commercial negotiations with supplier management for high-value and complex contracts. Negotiate pricing, escalation, payment terms, schedule commitments, milestones, warranties, remedies, change management, intellectual property, confidentiality, audit rights, flow-down requirements, and other commercial provisions. Use should-cost models, market intelligence, commodity and escalation indices, historical benchmarks, competitive analysis, and total-cost-of-ownership approaches to support fact-based negotiations and deliver measurable commercial value.
  • Risk, Compliance and Quality Requirements: Proactively identify and manage sourcing and supplier risks that could affect cost, schedule, quality, project execution, or regulatory compliance. Partner with Legal, Tax/Treasury, Credit Risk, Insurance, Cybersecurity, Engineering, Nuclear Oversight/Quality, and other subject matter experts to evaluate and address technical, contractual, financial, cybersecurity, domestic content, FEOC, IRA, prevailing wage/apprenticeship, logistics, quality, regulatory, and supplier capacity risks.
  • Supplier Qualification, Performance, and Corrective Action: Lead commercial and supply-chain assessments of suppliers and sub-suppliers, including evaluation of capability, quality programs, delivery performance, financial stability, capacity, workforce readiness, manufacturing capability, supply-chain resiliency, geopolitical exposure, documentation/traceability, and ability to support project milestones. Monitor supplier commitments and performance, support corrective actions, resolve escalated commercial issues, and communicate supplier risks through appropriate metrics and governance forums.
  • Business Partner, Project, and Stakeholder Management: Serve as a strategic supply chain partner to Nuclear Business Unit, Project Management & Construction (PMC), Nuclear Operations, Engineering, Project Controls, Legal, Finance, Tax, and project execution teams. Build strong internal and supplier relationships, translate technical and project requirements into effective sourcing strategies, facilitate cross-functional decision making, and develop solutions that balance technical, commercial, schedule, quality, and risk objectives.
  • Change Management and Continuous Improvement: Identify opportunities to improve sourcing processes, supplier engagement, project visibility, governance, and execution. Support implementation plans, lessons learned, digital reporting, sourcing metrics, supplier readiness reviews, templates, and tools that strengthen sourcing effectiveness and support consistent execution across potential new nuclear projects.
  • Leadership and Knowledge Sharing: Provide cross-training, coaching, and guidance to sourcing personnel and business partners. Share sourcing best practices and develop practical job aids, sourcing templates, supplier evaluation criteria, negotiation strategies, and procedural guidance. Contribute to the continued development of New Nuclear Generation sourcing capabilities and a strong culture of commercial discipline, collaboration, accountability, and continuous improvement.
